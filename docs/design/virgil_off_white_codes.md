# "CODES" c/o VIRGIL ABLOH™ — a reference for borrowing the grammar, not the marks

Research note for the mjzun.github.io portfolio. Compiled 2026-09-14 from ~30 sources (interviews, the Harvard GSD lecture/book, the MCA "Figures of Speech" catalogue coverage, trademark filings, and direct inspection of the live and archived HTML/CSS of off---white.com, canary---yellow.com, virgilabloh.com and free-game.virgilabloh.com).

Conventions used below:

- Quotes are verbatim as the cited source printed them. Where a quote reaches us second-hand (e.g. Highsnobiety quoting 032c), both hops are named.
- `VERIFIED` = I fetched the page/CSS myself. `REPORTED` = a secondary source says so and I could not check the primary.
- The point of this document is the *method* — quotation marks, hazard stripes, "c/o", ™, labels, exposed construction — not Off-White's registered marks. The "Do not copy" list at the end is not optional.

---

## 0. The method in one paragraph (so the codes make sense)

Abloh's whole visual language is downstream of one idea: take an existing thing, change it as little as possible, and let typography do the work of re-contextualising it. He called the source object a *readymade* (after Duchamp), the size of the change *the 3% approach*, and the typographic move *"quotes"*. Everything else — stripes, arrows, zip ties, "c/o", ™ — is the industrial/signage vernacular he used as the *material* for those edits. As he put it in the 2017 Harvard talk: "Things are intriguing to me when they're slightly edited, like these shoes … I was only interested in restraining myself, and only editing it 3 percent." ([Harvard Gazette, Nov 2017](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2017/11/harvard-welcomes-virgil-abloh-for-gsd-lecture/))

## 1. Quotation marks

### What he said (the WHY)

- On typography as the whole intervention: "You can use typography and wording to completely change the perception of a thing without changing anything about it. If I take a men's sweatshirt and write 'woman' on its back, that's art." — Abloh, quoted in [Highsnobiety, "Why Is Off-White Always Using Quotation Marks?" (2017)](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/virgil-abloh-off-white-quotation-marks/). Highsnobiety frames the device as *"ironic detachment"* — taking words "out of context" to make the viewer question the object.
- The legal-conceptual premise: "I often tell people that Duchamp is my lawyer. He's the legal premise to validate what I'm doing." … "Streetwear started from the gesture of taking a logo, flipping it upside down, and sewing it back on." … "This is a readymade. Nike designed the original in Oregon, and then… How do I make this shoe different?" — [032c, "VIRGIL ABLOH: 'Duchamp is my Lawyer'" (2018)](https://032c.com/magazine/duchamp-is-my-lawyer-virgil-abloh). In that same interview he asked that the words *streetwear* and *merchwear* be printed inside quotation marks in the magazine — the device applied to prose, not just product. ([nss magazine](https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/18495/virgil-abloh-copyright-quotes) and [032c](https://032c.com/magazine/duchamp-is-my-lawyer-virgil-abloh))
- Quotes and ™ as *graphics*, not punctuation: "On my Instagram, I often use quote marks or trademark symbols. I look at those as graphics." — Abloh to Hans Ulrich Obrist, [System Magazine, Issue 10](https://system-magazine.com/issues/issue-10/face-a-face-virgil-abloh-hans-ulrich-obrist). Same conversation: "My clothes are ironic. They are designed to make people question what else is in their closet."
- Irony as a generational stance (his own dictionary entry): **Irony** — "The philosophy of a new generation." From the Courier-typed pamphlet *The vocabulary according to Virgil Abloh* handed out at his LV SS19 debut. ([ZOE Magazine](https://www.zoemagazine.net/135739-virgil-ablohs-vocabulary-louis-vuitton-ss19-men/), [FASHION Magazine](https://fashionmagazine.com/style/virgil-abloh-louis-vuitton-debut/))
- The 2017 Columbia GSAPP lecture was literally titled *"Everything in Quotes"*; Abloh described it as the first attempt "to draw a link between all my creative endeavors using my Architecture and Structural Engineering formal education". ([Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2017/2/virgil-abloh-everything-in-quotes-columbia-university-lecture))
- The exhibition title is the same idea at museum scale: his use of language and quotation marks turns "the objects he designs and the people who wear his clothing into 'figures of speech.'" ([MCA Chicago](https://mcachicago.org/whats-on/exhibitions/virgil-abloh/), [Brooklyn Museum](https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/exhibitions/virgil_abloh), [LRB blog, Dec 2021](https://www.lrb.co.uk/blog/2021/december/figures-of-speech)). The LRB piece adds another Abloh line on captions-as-value: "an artwork for the art world is any idea attached to an invoice" (to *Flash Art*).
- How his own lawyers explained it to the USPTO: the quotation marks "add a layer of meaning to the phrase" and "transform the words used and create a unique, source-identifying commercial impression." ([The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/from-off-white-stripes-to-red-zip-ties-virgil-ablohs-approach-to-branding/))
- Critics' gloss, useful for designers: the quotes "call into question the source of a statement and the sincerity with which it was made" — "performative graffiti" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/viewpoints/virgil-abloh-figures-speech-mca-chicago/)); "redesign judo … forcing viewers to reevaluate an object with minimal intervention — just a couple of marks around a word" ([Fast Company, via search summary](https://www.fastcompany.com/90700911/virgil-abloh-conquered-the-world-in-air-quotes)); "the most misunderstood element" and "arguably the most intelligent" ([Open All Hours](https://www.openallhours.co/p/the-off-white-logo-system)).

## 2. Diagonal stripes

### Origin and rationale

- In his own words, the stripes are a borrowed *universal* sign and an architectural principle: "That's how I think of fashion and the branding, with these diagonal lines that are a universal language to represent hazard, or caution or something." … "A lot of that visual language was inspired by a principle of architecture." … "It's something that's just readily available, it's almost like you can't patent it in a way." — [Dezeen interview, June 2017](https://www.dezeen.com/2017/06/23/virgil-abloh-interview-mies-van-der-rohe-influence-off-white/) (on Mies / IIT's Crown Hall and the International Style: "one aesthetic can exist in different cultures").
- The stripes as a *unifier*: "The diagonal lines help me solve a huge issue, how to take a bunch of random things and let them be one. That's the mindset of Helvetica. That's the mindset of architecture." — Abloh in 032c, as quoted by [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/virgil-abloh-off-white-quotation-marks/) and [TypeRoom](https://www.typeroom.eu/its-the-font-that-really-matters-a-tribute-to-typophile-creative-trailblazer-virgil-abloh).
- Vernacular source: pedestrian crossings, road signs, hazard tape, loading bays — "the graphic you find on cross-walks and on road signs and have found there for many decades before Off-White's inception" ([The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/virgil-ablohs-off-white-logos-are-everywhere-but-are-they-worthy-of-trademark-protection/)); "reference hazard tape and loading bays rather than ornamental design" ([Open All Hours](https://www.openallhours.co/p/the-off-white-logo-system)). Complex's branding analysis: Abloh chose a deliberately *unoriginal* mark so the brand could "immediately benefit from an already-established icon" ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/style/a/cameron-wolf/off-white-virgil-abloh-branding-diagonal-lines), [TFL](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/from-off-white-stripes-to-red-zip-ties-virgil-ablohs-approach-to-branding/)).
- Probable specific precedent (REPORTED, credible): Kinneir Calvert Associates' 1964–65 Glasgow Airport identity paired the crossed-arrows symbol with vehicles and flight ladders painted with "yellow striped tops so that they are clearly visible from the air" ([Grailed, "Behind the Design"](https://www.grailed.com/drycleanonly/off-white-graphic-design-history); [Hypebeast on Diet Prada's investigation](https://hypebeast.com/2018/6/off-white-logo-origins-diet-prada-glasgow-airport)). Abloh never publicly responded to the Diet Prada post.

### Proportions and use

- Trademark filings describe the mark, not a ratio: "fifteen alternating parallel diagonal lines of varying sizes forming the shape of a rectangle" (Class 35), "fifteen … forming the shape of a square" (Classes 18/25), and "nineteen alternating parallel diagonal lines of varying sizes forming the shape of a rectangle" — all filed July 2016 ([The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/virgil-ablohs-off-white-logos-are-everywhere-but-are-they-worthy-of-trademark-protection/)). Note "varying sizes": the outer lines are clipped by the bounding box, so stripe *count inside a fixed rectangle* is the spec, not a fixed stripe width.
- Angle: REPORTED as 45° by authentication guides and logo write-ups ([Rebag](https://www.rebag.com/thevault/off-white-101-virgil-abloh-launches-a-revolution/), [designyourway](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/off-white-logo/)); Abloh never stated an angle. Visually it reads as 45° with roughly equal black/white stripe widths.
- Two modes: (a) **field/pattern** — a filled rectangle or square of stripes on the back of hoodies, on the "Diag" line, on bags; (b) **border/band** — on sleeves, hems, straps, belts, and the edges of packaging ([TFL](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/from-off-white-stripes-to-red-zip-ties-virgil-ablohs-approach-to-branding/): "appear on sleeves, backs of garments, bags, and coats"). Colour is usually black/white; yellow/black and white-on-colour variants exist (the "Diag" name is the product-line name).
- In the MCA show: Miesian I-beams "painted with safety stripping" under the title *Manchester, Illinois* ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/viewpoints/virgil-abloh-figures-speech-mca-chicago/)).

## 3. Arrows, ™, "c/o", zip ties, tabs, exposed construction, and the "cheat codes"

### Crossed arrows

- Four arrows converging/diverging on a St Andrew's cross. Traced by Diet Prada (June 2018) to Kinneir Calvert Associates' Glasgow Airport symbol (1964–65), "reminiscent of the St Andrew's Cross, with arrows pointing in and out"; Abloh was photographed with Taschen's *Logo Modernism*, which contains the Glasgow entry. ([Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2018/6/off-white-logo-origins-diet-prada-glasgow-airport), [Pause](https://pausemag.co.uk/2018/06/diet-prada-investigates-off-whites-crossed-arrows-logo-origins/), [Grailed](https://www.grailed.com/drycleanonly/off-white-graphic-design-history)). Coincidence worth knowing: Calvert's *Rail Alphabet* typeface's lightest weight is named "Off White" ([Grailed](https://www.grailed.com/drycleanonly/off-white-graphic-design-history), [1000logos](https://1000logos.net/off-white-logo/)).
- Function over decoration: the arrows read "like infrastructure — directional, functional, almost instructional" ([Open All Hours](https://www.openallhours.co/p/the-off-white-logo-system)).
- In Oct 2019 he swapped the arrows for a new mark described in the trademark filing as "a sinking person's hands and face below the words 'Off' and 'White'", captioned on Instagram only "updated identity. miscellaneous images." ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/style/2019/10/virgil-abloh-unveils-new-off-white-logo-design)). Lesson: he treated even the logo as replaceable content.

### "™" and "c/o"

- Full legal name of the label: **OFF-WHITE c/o VIRGIL ABLOH™**. "c/o" is postal "care of" — the brand is addressed *to the attention of* a person, which frames the designer as a temporary custodian rather than an owner ([madegooddesigns](https://madegooddesigns.com/off-white-brand-guidelines/), [Open All Hours](https://www.openallhours.co/p/the-off-white-logo-system)). The convention is reused for collaborations — "Off-White™ c/o AC Milan", "L/AB c/o Off-White™" (VERIFIED on the live site footer: "Off-White™ and L/AB c/o Off-White™ are trademarks owned by Off-White LLC.").
- ™ on everything, including his own name ("Virgil Abloh™" is the `<title>` of the 2019 archive site — VERIFIED via Wayback) — and he says he reads it as a graphic: "I look at those as graphics." ([System Magazine](https://system-magazine.com/issues/issue-10/face-a-face-virgil-abloh-hans-ulrich-obrist)). Fast Company's reading: "a wink and a nod from Abloh that he knew that you knew that this whole thing was a construct."

### The zip tie, orange tabs, exposed foam, text where it doesn't belong (Nike "The Ten", 2017)

- The Ten was organised in two groups: **"REVEALING"** — "hand-cut, open-source and reconstructed" (Air Jordan 1, Air Max 90, Presto, VaporMax, Blazer) — and **"GHOSTING"** — translucent uppers (AF1, Air Max 97, Chuck 70, Hyperdunk, Vaporfly). ([Nike newsroom](https://about.nike.com/en/newsroom/releases/virgil-abloh-nike-the-10), [Nike magazine](https://about.nike.com/en/magazine/virgil-abloh-nike-the-ten-history))
- The X-ACTO knife was the tool: cutting the tongue to expose foam, moving the Swoosh, adding "pops of color through orange tabs". Text: "AIR" on the air unit, "SHOELACES" on laces, and the interior print "Off-White for Nike / [shoe name] / Beaverton, Oregon USA / c. 2017" — the caption *is* the design. ([Nike](https://about.nike.com/en/magazine/virgil-abloh-nike-the-ten-history), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_and_Off-White:_'The_Ten'))
- Why: "If there is an airbag, then I'm going to write the word 'AIR' on it." … "In one gesture, I wanted to underscore how the design system and manufacturing of Nike are so perfect." … "Yes, we're making a desired product, but by making a trip to your local store, and using tools you have at home, you could also make this shoe." … "I spend a tremendous amount of time thinking about the kid in the middle of nowhere who has a passion for design, much like I did." ([Nike](https://about.nike.com/en/magazine/virgil-abloh-nike-the-ten-history))
- The zip tie: red (widely called orange; the USPTO filing says "a zip tie with a substantially rectangular end, all in the color red"), printed in white with the date, the collaboration name and the word "zip-tie"; used since 2016; refused as "functional" repeatedly, registered March 2022 ([The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/from-zip-ties-to-quotation-marks-a-look-at-off-whites-quest-for-exclusive-rights/), [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/style/off-white-zip-tie-trademark-registration), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_and_Off-White:_'The_Ten')). His counsel's argument is a design brief in itself: consumers "leave [the zip tie] affixed to the outside of the product, even though it could be easily removed." Abloh's only public rationale is that extra laces and ties were included so "people might make their own Off-White™ designs" ([Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/virgil-abloh-off-white-nikes-customization/)); Highsnobiety's own gloss ties it to his architecture/industrial background ([Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/virgil-abloh-off-white-zip-ties/)).

### The "cheat codes" (Harvard GSD, "Insert Complicated Title Here", 26 Oct 2017)

No single public source prints the full numbered list verbatim; the transcript is the Sternberg/Harvard GSD book (ed. Jennifer Sigler & Leah Whitman-Salkin, design Åbäke, 96 pp, ISBN 978-3-95679-381-3, May 2018 — [GSD](https://www.gsd.harvard.edu/publication/insert-complicated-title-here/), [Sternberg](https://www.sternberg-press.com/product/virgil-abloh/)). The framing quotes are "What's my DNA?" (opening) and "What's your DNA?" (closing), and, on obstacles, "They literally don't exist." The codes as reconstructed from [Wallpaper*](https://www.wallpaper.com/architecture/virgil-abloh-the-incidents-book-harvard-gsd-sternberg-press), [Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/fashion/article/55641/1/virgil-ablohs-commandments-for-hacking-creativity), [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2017/11/harvard-welcomes-virgil-abloh-for-gsd-lecture/), and later summaries ([we-rule](https://we-rule.com/blog/2025/12/4/the-virgil-abloh-cheat-codes-7-rules-to-bypass-gatekeepers-from-his-legendary-harvard-lecture), [Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/design-philosophy-virgil-abloh)):

1. **Readymade** — "As designers and artists we exist as a result of the many iterations before us" (Wallpaper*); "nothing is new and everything's a remix – life as a DJ set" (Dazed paraphrase); "We exist off the backs of many other things and iterations before us… It's about shortcuts." (we-rule).
2. **"Duchamp is my lawyer"** — the legal/art-historical premise for #1 (032c, 2018; the phrase is his, the lecture slide is REPORTED).
3. **The 3% approach** — "I was interested in restraining myself and only editing it three per cent" (Wallpaper*); "If you put two percent more, or you take away two percent, that changes something completely… My cheat code has been three percent." (Blake Crosley's transcription). His LV dictionary later defines **3%** as "The exact ratio needed to twist a normative object into something special." (ZOE).
4. **"Quotes"** — speak in quotation marks; irony as a way to critique a system while participating in it (we-rule, "Speak in 'Quotes'").
5. **Signs of "work in progress"** — "Once I realized that it's okay not to be a perfectionist, I could suddenly do a million things" (Wallpaper*); we-rule's version adds "…and go to sleep at night."
6. **A societal commentary / the "trash bin" test** — "Any output has to have a reason to exist… Does it need to exist? It's okay to say no." (Wallpaper*).
7. **Tourist vs. purist** — "Tourist and purist — that's my main device to understand the sections of culture, that move culture forward." ([Rainbowed](https://www.rainbowed.us/art-design/all-the-things-virgil-abloh-said/); expanded in [capturetheessence](https://capturetheessence.blog/luxury-landscape/how-virgil-ablohs-tourist-vs-purist-helps-us-understand-fashion-consumers/): the purist "know[s] the whole art history of everything", the tourist is "bright-eyed, curiosity-driven"). The goal is to "Speak to the tourist and purist simultaneously" ([Ikono](https://ikono.org/the-vision-behind-the-hype-virgil-abloh-in-5-quotes/)).
8. **Question your mentors / question everything** — "I have mentors who are dead. I have mentors 30 years older than me. I have mentors 10 years younger" (Harvard Gazette); the "Question Everything" flag flew outside the MCA in 2019 ([Refinery29](https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/2022/07/11034481/virgil-abloh-figures-of-speech-brooklyn-museum-review)).
9. **Failure is a ghost** — "There is no failure. Failure is as real as Halloween ghosts… I can just put work out." (we-rule); "I'm into failing — it's cool. It's part of my trajectory." (Rainbowed).
10. **"Everything I do is for the 17-year-old version of myself."** ([Dazed quotes roundup](https://www.dazeddigital.com/fashion/article/54932/1/virgil-abloh-inspiring-powerful-quotes-louis-vuitton-off-white)); closing line of the Harvard talk: "Put yourself in my shoes. I'm not that special." (Harvard Gazette).
11. **Figures of speech** — the umbrella term he later used for the whole practice: objects and wearers become "figures of speech" (MCA, 2019).
12. **Streetwear = "adding contemporary value"** — positioning streetwear as the current movement after modern/contemporary art (Wallpaper*); "Design is like DJing. You take existing sounds and recontextualise them." ([Istituto Marangoni](https://www.istitutomarangoni.com/en/maze35/game-changers/virgil-abloh-3-percent-rule-fashion)).

## 4. Typography

### The cut

- Every credible source names **Helvetica, bold, all caps** as the label's type; some specify Helvetica Bold (Linotype) for the wordmark ([madegooddesigns](https://madegooddesigns.com/off-white-font/), [designyourway](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/what-font-does-off-white-use/), [TypeRoom](https://www.typeroom.eu/its-the-font-that-really-matters-a-tribute-to-typophile-creative-trailblazer-virgil-abloh)). "Helvetica Neue Bold" is often claimed but I found no primary confirmation; on garments the differences are invisible at label sizes anyway.
- VERIFIED from CSS: the live off---white.com self-hosts two faces registered as `font-family: Helvetica` and `Helvetica-Bold` (same woff2 file for both declarations), with tokens `--font-regular: 'Helvetica'`, `--font-medium: 'Helvetica-Bold'`, `--font-tertiary(-bold)`. The 2019 canary---yellow.com theme embedded **HelveticaNeueLT-Roman** (`font-family: 'Helvetica'`), plus Courier Prime, FreeMono and Windsor Light. So: Helvetica (Neue, Roman/Bold) for labels; a monospace for "typed" content.
- Why Helvetica: "I like Helvetica. It's the most generic font there is. It keeps an even keel." ([System Magazine](https://system-magazine.com/issues/issue-10/face-a-face-virgil-abloh-hans-ulrich-obrist)); the 032c line above — "That's the mindset of Helvetica." Third-party gloss: "ubiquitous to the point of being invisible" ([madegooddesigns](https://madegooddesigns.com/off-white-font/)).
- Instagram, Dec 2021 memorial round-ups: "It's the font that really matters." ([TypeRoom](https://www.typeroom.eu/its-the-font-that-really-matters-a-tribute-to-typophile-creative-trailblazer-virgil-abloh))

### Setting

- All caps, straight quotes, tight-to-normal tracking, black on white or white on black. Text runs *along* things (laces, midsoles, seams, straps) and *inside* things (tongue interiors, bag linings), i.e. placement follows the object's own edges. Hierarchy on product: a large object-name label ("SCULPTURE"), a mid-size attribution line (OFF-WHITE c/o VIRGIL ABLOH™ / "Off-White for Nike"), and a fine-print line (place, year: "Beaverton, Oregon USA c. 2017"; "MADE IN ITALY"-style). Sizes are not published; the three-tier structure is the consistent thing.
- Monospace/"typed" for documents: the LV SS19 *vocabulary* pamphlet was "typed-out in Courier font" ([FASHION Magazine](https://fashionmagazine.com/style/virgil-abloh-louis-vuitton-debut/)); the 2019 archive site loaded Courier Prime/FreeMono; virgilabloh.com now uses "Simon Mono" (VERIFIED). Pattern: Helvetica for *labels*, monospace for *notes/receipts/definitions*.
- Serif as the counter-voice: for the website and newer branding Off-White commissioned (with Studio Temp as branding agency) a custom cut of **Bookish** by Niklas Ekholm / Helsinki Type Studio — "a more defined and regular structure", Renaissance-antiqua proportions, redrawn numerals, made to survive "embossing on rubber soles, weaving into fabric labels, and printing on packaging" ([Helsinki Type Studio](https://helsinkitypestudio.com/custom/off-white), [Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/typefaces/159383/bookish)). VERIFIED in CSS as `Bookish-Book` and `Bookish-Off-White` (`--font-secondary`, `--font-secondary-brand`), alongside `IBMPlexMono-Regular/Medium`.

### The live off---white.com (VERIFIED 2026-09-14, from `/mobify/bundle/5607/main.css` and page HTML)

- Fonts: Helvetica / Helvetica-Bold (labels, nav, buttons), Bookish-Book / Bookish-Off-White (editorial/brand), IBM Plex Mono (data). Uppercase is used 231 times in the sheet; letter-spacing tokens are small positives: 0.36, 0.42, 0.48, 0.54, 0.6, 0.72, 0.96 px (i.e. 3–8% of a 12px cap). Type scale: 0.625 / 0.75 / 0.875 / 1 / 1.125 / 1.25 / 1.5 / 2 rem — 0.875rem is the workhorse.
- Colour tokens: `--white-color: #fff`, black `#000000`, near-blacks `#171717` / `#1D1D1B`, greys `#9e9e9e #e5e5e5 #eeeeee #f5f5f5`, an actual off-white/beige `#E2DBC8`, an electric blue `#0019e9` / `#0a20e8`, and — this is the tell — `--warning-color: #ffff00`.
- Chrome: `border: 1px solid var(--color-black)` is the default control edge (12 uses), links `text-decoration: underline` (34) with `line-through` (11) for struck prices; hovers are flat colour swaps (`:hover{color:var(--color-white)}`, `:hover{color:var(--color-black)}`), no shadows. Nav is uppercase category labels: NEW IN / MEN / WOMEN / SNEAKERS / ACCESSORIES / KIDS / BRAND / 10x10 Off-White™. Grid is a product-first, boxed layout (tokens `xxl`, `total`, `offset`).
- The 2018 site (Wayback, June 2018, VERIFIED): `<title>Off-white</title>`, tagline "Defining the grey area between black and white", nav items rendered *in quotation marks* — "WEBSITE", "FINE PRINT", "Options" — plus sections FURNITURE / PHYSICAL RETAIL / SOUND / VIDEO / HOW TO. Cart label: "Shopping Bag:". That is the quotation-mark device applied to UI chrome, straight from the source.

Two things to internalise:

1. **"Off-white" the colour is not the point.** The name is a position, not a swatch: "Off-White is about the grey area between white and black" ([Jarrett Fuller quoting Abloh](https://www.jarrettfuller.blog/2019/07/virgil-abloh/)); the brand "defines the grey area between black and white as a color" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Off-White-company)); "The name itself is a contradiction." ([032c](https://032c.com/magazine/duchamp-is-my-lawyer-virgil-abloh)). The live site's actual "off-white" (`#E2DBC8`) is a minor token. The visual system is high-contrast black/white with hazard accents.
2. **Canary Yellow is a name, not a background.** canary---yellow.com's page background is `#FFFFFF` with `#1E1E1C` text in Helvetica/Arial and "Simon Mono" for mono; the yellow `#FFEF2C` is an accent. The 2019 original (Studio Temp) was black Helvetica Neue on white — no yellow ground at all (VERIFIED via Wayback CSS). The yellow lineage is THE BRILLIANCE! blog (2005–), which was "black font on white background, yellow highlights, 100x100 pixel images, and lots of exclamation marks" ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/style/a/noah-johnson/the-brilliance-is-back-what-is-the-brilliance)). The Fashion Law traces the name's trademark history (a FUBU subsidiary tried "Canary Yellow" in 2003 and was refused as merely descriptive) ([TFL](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/from-fubu-to-virgil-abloh-the-legal-history-of-canary-yellow/)).

## 6. Signage and industrial vernacular

- **Arrows** — wayfinding (see §3). **Hazard/"CAUTION" tape** — the stripes as tape: yellow barricade tape is one of the label's "signature marks" ([Rebag](https://www.rebag.com/thevault/off-white-101-virgil-abloh-launches-a-revolution/)); at the MCA, structural I-beams got safety striping ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/viewpoints/virgil-abloh-figures-speech-mca-chicago/)).
- **"OFF" stencil** — the wordmark split into OFF / WHITE and set in a stencil/cross-bar cut on many pieces (e.g. the "Impressionism Diagonal Stencil Hoodie", [Lyst](https://www.lyst.com/clothing/off-white-co-virgil-abloh-black-impressionism-diagonal-stencil-hoodie/)); stencil = spray-through-a-template = construction-site lettering. (REPORTED via product listings; Abloh gave no quote on the stencil specifically.)
- **"Blue Collar" (SS16)** — "crashing a white collar aesthetic with a blue collar aesthetic and seeing what the result of that is"; tees for a fictional "Off-White Construction" carried a working phone number (1-855-633-9483) to a voicemail of Abloh explaining the collection — "a way to add color to the clothes" ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/style/a/cameron-wolf/virgil-abloh-explains-off-white-collection-at-working-number)). The industrial vernacular is not styling; it's the *subject*.
- **Mies / IIT** — "Studying architecture in Crown Hall had a lasting effect on my aesthetic" ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2017/06/23/virgil-abloh-interview-mies-van-der-rohe-influence-off-white/)); the Free Game site's bio credits IIT's Mies-derived Bauhaus curriculum for teaching him "to combine the fields of art, craft and design" (VERIFIED, page source).
- **"IMPRESSIONISM" (SS19)** — Manet's *Monet Painting on His Studio Boat* (1874) printed on hoodies and tees with diagonals, arrows and "signature slogans in inverted commas"; the title word itself is set as a label ([Steemit/lyfstyl](https://steemit.com/fashion/@lyfstyl/virgilablohoff-whitelooktoimpressionistart-tps92zlun5), [eBay listing](https://www.ebay.com/itm/324940419870)). Pattern: a canonical artwork treated as a readymade, captioned with its art-history category.
- **"Meteor Shower"** — the Jitney bag and boots perforated with crater holes, "rendering its function obsolete"; part of a Spring 2020 idea that a woman "can withstand a 'meteor shower' both metaphorically and physically speaking" ([WWD](https://wwd.com/runway/spring-ready-to-wear-2020/paris/off-white/review/), [BoF](https://www.businessoffashion.com/reviews/fashion-week/art-meets-science-at-off-white-spring/)).
- **"DEFINITION" pieces** — the strongest primary example is the LV SS19 pamphlet *"The vocabulary according to Virgil Abloh: a liberal definition of terms and explanation of ideas"*, an A–Z of Courier-typed definitions ("3%", "Irony", "Designer", "After party", "Normcore", "Utility") ([FASHION Magazine](https://fashionmagazine.com/style/virgil-abloh-louis-vuitton-debut/), [ZOE](https://www.zoemagazine.net/135739-virgil-ablohs-vocabulary-louis-vuitton-ss19-men/)). The book *Abloh-isms* (Princeton, 2021) continues the aphorism-as-artefact format ([Princeton UP](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691213798/abloh-isms)). I could not verify a specific Off-White garment titled "DEFINITION"; treat that as a family name for the dictionary-style typographic works.
- **Receipts and fine print** — the IKEA MARKERAD receipt rug ([Azure](https://www.azuremagazine.com/article/virgil-abloh-ikea-markerad/)); interior "Beaverton, Oregon USA c. 2017" prints; "FINE PRINT" as a *section* of the 2018 website (VERIFIED). Provenance data is treated as decoration.
- **Exhibition graphics** — the fifty-foot "Question Everything" flag; "LEWIS VUITTON" label on a North Face jacket; scaffolding and pink insulation foam as materials ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/viewpoints/virgil-abloh-figures-speech-mca-chicago/), [Refinery29](https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/2022/07/11034481/virgil-abloh-figures-of-speech-brooklyn-museum-review)); at Brooklyn the show used deliberately minimal wall text so viewers could choose to be "tourist or a purist".

## 7. Websites: Canary Yellow, Free Game, off---white.com, and the archive-as-portfolio idea

### The stance

- "My brand is digital; it came from Instagram. People know the brand through images." — [System Magazine](https://system-magazine.com/issues/issue-10/face-a-face-virgil-abloh-hans-ulrich-obrist). Also: "I want these interviews that we're doing together to be a sort of published research, a manifesto."
- The archive site was conceived as a book that became a website: "initially supposed to be translated into a book, but instead became a digital alternative accessible everywhere … a hybrid between an open portfolio and a collection of personal witnesses" ([nss magazine](https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/17436/virgil-abloh-archive-website)). Launched 22 Jan 2019, designed by Bergamo's Studio Temp, "channels Virgil's early THE BRILLIANCE! project with a clean, minimalist aesthetic"; contents: comprehensive archive, bio, mixes, "Televised Radio", events calendar, the Pyrex "A Team with No Sport" lookbook, the SS14 Off-White imagery, and the Harvard lecture recording ([Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2019/1/virgil-abloh-canary-yellow-website-launch)).

### canary---yellow.com / virgilabloh.com (VERIFIED)

- **2019 build (Wayback, WordPress theme "virgilabloh" by "Dev AM")**: `<title>Virgil Abloh™</title>`; `body { font-family: 'Helvetica' }` mapped to HelveticaNeueLT-Roman; text `#000`, background `#fff`; secondary faces Courier Prime, FreeMono, Windsor Light (a `.small-description` class); sizes in viewport units (`3vw`, `12vw`, `8vh`), uppercase used sparingly. Structure: a three-column archive (`.column-1/2/3`) with an "open all / close all" toggle, "More images" per project, categories as coloured buttons (Artwork, Design, Video, Press, Printed Matter, Lecture, Fashion, Special project, DJing, Architecture, Set Design — button colours `#FF1493`, `#ADFF2F`, `#34c4fa`), a "PRESS TO START" splash, a link "open comprehensive archive", and a header widget showing the visitor's **IP, city, local time and temperature** (`#ip`, `#address`, `.temperatura`, `#weather`) — the site labelled *you* the way he labelled objects. Bio subsections: Accolades, Biography, Exhibitions, Philosophy, Signature, Timeline.
- **2026 canary---yellow.com** (Shopify "Canary Yellow [Art Store]"): `--color-contrast: #FFFFFF`, `--color-primary: #1E1E1C`, `--font-body-family: Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`, `--font-mono: "Simon Mono"`, `--text-copy: 12px`, `letter-spacing: var(--tracking-tight)`, `--spacing-edge: 20px`, accent `#FFEF2C`, greys `#F6F6F6 / #F3F3F3 / #787876`. Logo is split text "Canary / Yellow" with "Art Store" between. Products are labelled by type in caps — BOOK, TEE, HAT — next to names like `V.A.A. Nike "LOGO" Tee`.
- **virgilabloh.com (2026, The Virgil Abloh Archive)**: Next.js; background `#1C4A96`, accent `#B9E7FE`, fonts "Simon Mono", "Business System", Helvetica; copy uses "V.A.A. ARCHIVE", "S-26", "V. 001" — version numbers as headings.
- **Domain grammar**: `off---white.com`, `canary---yellow.com`, `free---game.com` — the triple hyphen is his URL-level tic (no public rationale found; the practical reason is that the single-hyphen domains were taken, and the triple reads as a typographic dash). Subdomain-as-project: `post-modern.virgilabloh.com` ("POSTMODERN" scholarship), `free-game.virgilabloh.com`, `gallery.canary---yellow.com`.

### Free Game (VERIFIED, free-game.virgilabloh.com; mirror free---game.com)

- His intro copy: "Below please find a resource center for brands in their earliest phases. This resource was born as I was working to summarize answers to all my most frequently asked questions. The goal of this resource is to uplift more than a few. I will continue to open doors for those that come from the fringe and help them be awarded opportunities usually left for the center."
- Structure is a single long page, uppercase Helvetica headings, numbered like a manual: FROM A – Z (TABLE OF CONTENTS) → INTRODUCTION → HOW TO NAME YOUR BRAND → HOW TO OBTAIN A TRADEMARK → HOW TO USE ADOBE CREATIVE SUITE → HOW TO MAKE A SCREEN PRINT → HOW TO FIND BLANKS → HOW TO SHOOT A LOOKBOOK → HOW TO MAKE A WEBSITE → HOW TO ADD SHOPIFY → OVERARCHING LECTURES → FIGURES OF SPEECH CATALOGUE → PERSONAL MENTORS → FURTHER INSPIRATION. Each chapter = one paragraph by Abloh + one embedded YouTube tutorial by someone else (readymade pedagogy).
- On websites specifically, his whole position is instrumental: "When a lookbook is created, it will need to be distributed publically – To do this, a website is needed. Follow this video to create a website for your brand and your products." (the embedded video is a Squarespace tutorial). On naming: "Your brand name should be an endless reference point to why your brand exists."
- Launch coverage and quotes: "a mass form of mentorship to give information and access to black POC and to all those that are interested"; "for anyone reading this, finish it and then make shit happen, give it to your friends and then boom, you've entered the domino effect phase"; "Go and print that T-shirt today, and by today I mean in the next 30 minutes." ([Creative Lives in Progress](https://creativelivesinprogress.com/articles/resources-for-emerging-creatives-by-virgil-abloh), [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/virgil-abloh-free-game-mentorship/), [Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2020/10/virgil-abloh-free-game-mentorship-brand-startup-series)).

### off---white.com

See §4 for the verified type/colour/hover system. The two lessons for a portfolio: (1) in 2018 the *nav itself* was in quotes ("WEBSITE", "FINE PRINT") and the site had non-commerce sections (SOUND, VIDEO, HOW TO, FURNITURE) — the shop was framed as an archive; (2) in 2026 the chrome is 1px black rules, uppercase Helvetica labels, underline links, flat hover swaps, and a serif (Bookish) only where the brand "speaks".

### The archive-as-portfolio idea, distilled

Abloh's sites are not case-study decks. They are **inventories**: a flat list of everything, tagged by category, dated, with the meta-data (place, year, edition, version) shown as content. The tone is a museum label crossed with a receipt. The visitor is addressed as "tourist or purist" — minimal explanation, maximum access ("open all"). That is the portable idea.

## Translation table — code → legal web-UI move for `PORTFOLIO c/o MATTHEW JAY ZUNIGA`

| Abloh code | The idea behind it | Concrete, legal web-UI move |
|---|---|---|
| Quotation marks | Name the thing literally; the caption is the design; straight quotes, all caps | Every section title, image caption, and control label sits in straight typographic quotes: `"WORK"`, `"ABOUT"`, `"IMAGE"`, `"BUTTON"`, `"FOOTER"`. Implement as a `::before/::after` with `content:"\0022"` on a `.q` utility so the quotes are consistent and copy-paste clean. Use the literal generic name of the element, never a slogan. |
| Labels on the wrong surface ("AIR" on the airbag) | Reveal what the object is made of | Show provenance as UI: `"HTML"` `"CSS"` `"JS 12KB"` in the footer; `"IMAGE" 1600×1000 · JPG · 210KB` under every figure; `"BUILT" 2026-09` on cards. Expose the `<figure>`/`<figcaption>` structure rather than hiding it. |
| "c/o" | Custodian, not owner; collaboration syntax | Masthead reads `PORTFOLIO c/o MATTHEW JAY ZUNIGA`. Project pages read `"PROJECT NAME" c/o CLIENT` or `c/o SELF`. Use the same syntax for credits: `TYPE c/o [foundry]`. |
| ™ on everything | Marks as graphics; deadpan officialdom | A superscript `™` after the site name and after your own name in the footer, set in the same Helvetica at ~60% size. Do not trademark-symbol other people's names. |
| Diagonal hazard stripes | Universal caution/attention sign; a unifier for "random things" | `background: repeating-linear-gradient(45deg, #000 0 12px, #fff 12px 24px)` as (a) a 24px border on focus rings / the active nav item / the dock, (b) a full-bleed divider between sections, (c) a "WET PAINT"-style band on work-in-progress items. Keep it black/white; use yellow/black only for a single "warning" state. **Never** as a bounded rectangle/square logo device (that is the registered mark, see below). |
| Crossed arrows | Wayfinding, infrastructure | Use *real* wayfinding glyphs, not their logo: `→` `↗` `↩` from the font, an "EXIT" link, "YOU ARE HERE" on the current nav item. |
| Zip tie / orange tab | A removable, deliberately-left-on tag of authenticity | A small orange-red pill on new items reading `"NEW" · 2026-09` that the user can dismiss (and that stays if they don't). A sticky "tag" element on the corner of the hero: `"PORTFOLIO" · v.001 · c. 2026`. |
| Exposed construction (cut tongue, visible foam, stitching) | Show the guts | Leave 1px black rules visible around every layout region; render an optional "show grid" toggle; show `<details>` with the raw markup of a component; expose alt text as a visible caption on hover. |
| 3% approach / readymade | Start from a default and barely edit it | Use system Helvetica/Arial and browser-default form controls; edit them ~3% (a 1px black border, uppercase label, straight quotes). The "design" is the labelling, not a bespoke component library. |
| Helvetica, all caps, tight tracking, three-tier labels | Neutral type, hierarchy by size only | `font-family: Helvetica, "Helvetica Neue", Arial, sans-serif`; labels `text-transform: uppercase; letter-spacing: 0.04em`; three sizes only: 0.75rem (fine print), 0.875rem (labels), 2rem+ (object names). Bold for the object name, regular for everything else. |
| Monospace for documents / definitions | Receipts, dictionaries, typed notes | An "ABOUT" page written as an A–Z of definitions in a monospace face (IBM Plex Mono / Courier Prime), e.g. `"PORTFOLIO" — a list, not a pitch.` |
| Serif as counter-voice (Bookish) | One elegant face used only where the brand speaks | One serif (e.g. a licensed or open Renaissance antiqua) reserved for a single tagline or the colophon; everything else sans. |
| Colour: black/white + hazard accent | Signage, not palette | Tokens: `--ink:#000; --paper:#fff; --caution:#FFEF2C or #FFFF00; --tab:#FE3E0D; --grey:#A5A5A5; --mid-grey:#E6E6E6`. No gradients, no shadows, hover = flat colour swap. The one "off-white" `#E2DBC8` for the page background if you want the pun — but see §5.2. |
| Fine print / receipt data | Provenance as decoration | Footer set as a receipt: `"PORTFOLIO" · MATTHEW JAY ZUNIGA™ · [CITY] · c. 2026 · VER. 001 · LAST BUILD [date] · [bytes]`. |
| Visitor-as-object (2019 archive's IP/weather widget) | Label the viewer too | A header line: `"VISITOR" · [local time] · [viewport w×h] · [prefers-color-scheme]` computed client-side; no IP lookups, no third-party calls. |
| Tourist / purist | Two audiences at once | Every project card has a one-line "tourist" caption and an expandable `"NOTES"` `<details>` for the purist (stack, constraints, what changed). "open all / close all" toggle at page top, exactly like the 2019 archive. |
| Archive, not case studies | Flat inventory, categories, dates | Home = one long list of everything, sortable by category buttons (`"WEB"`, `"PRINT"`, `"CODE"`, `"WRITING"`), each row dated. No hero carousel. |
| "Question Everything" / "Work in progress" | Publish unfinished | A `"WORK IN PROGRESS"` hazard-striped band on live items; a public changelog page. |
| Triple-hyphen URL | Typographic tic at the address level | Optional and legal: a `---` separator in path slugs (`/work---2026/`), never in the domain if it would imitate `off---white.com`. |
| Free Game "HOW TO" | Give the method away | A `"HOW TO"` page: how this site is built, with the source linked. |

## Do not copy (marks with live registrations or obvious trade dress)

- **The wordmark** "OFF-WHITE", "Off-White™", "OFF-WHITE c/o VIRGIL ABLOH", "L/AB c/o Off-White™" (owned by Off-White LLC; VERIFIED footer notice). Do not set your name as `OFF-…` anything.
- **The crossed-arrows logo** and the 2019 "sinking hands and face" mark ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/style/2019/10/virgil-abloh-unveils-new-off-white-logo-design)).
- **The diagonal-stripe device as a bounded square/rectangle logo** — the 15- and 19-line marks are registered for apparel, bags and retail services ([TFL](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/virgil-ablohs-off-white-logos-are-everywhere-but-are-they-worthy-of-trademark-protection/)). Hazard striping as a *border/divider* is generic vernacular; the *bounded logo block* is not.
- **The red zip tie with rectangular end** — registered March 2022 ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/style/off-white-zip-tie-trademark-registration)). Don't render a zip-tie graphic as a brand tag.
- **"PRODUCT BAG" in quotes** (allowed by USPTO Dec 2020) and other Off-White word marks ("FOR WALKING" was applied for). Quotation marks *as a device* are not protectable; those specific phrases on those goods are.
- **Nike marks** (Swoosh, "AIR", "Jordan", "Beaverton, Oregon" trade dress from The Ten), **Louis Vuitton** (LV monogram, "LEWIS VUITTON" parody), **IKEA** ("MARKERAD", the blue FRAKTA bag shape).
- **"Canary Yellow"**, "V.A.A.", "Virgil Abloh™", "Figures of Speech", "Free Game" as names; the Studio Temp layout and the Bookish-Off-White typeface files. Do not use Abloh's name or likeness in your masthead.
- Don't reproduce the Manet/Monet "IMPRESSIONISM" print compositions or any Off-White graphic art verbatim.

What *is* free: straight quotation marks, "c/o", ™ on your own name, all-caps Helvetica, 45° black/white hazard striping as a border, orange tags, exposed rules, receipts, dictionaries, an inventory layout, and the method itself (readymade + 3% + label).
